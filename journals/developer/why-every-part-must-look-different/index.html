Why Every Part of Attoo Must Look Different

I made a mistake in the first version of Attoo's subpages. I created the routes, divided the subjects, and filled them with useful sentences. Technically, the sitemap worked. Emotionally, every room felt the same.

The developer page, directive, graveyard, research files, and glossary were variations of one container. That structure made the content orderly, but flattened the meaning.

Format Is Part of Memory

An autobiography should not feel like a database result. It needs room for embarrassment, contradiction, and a voice that does not arrive in equal-sized cards. The developer page therefore became a worn paper file: something handled, revised, and never completely clean.

The directive is different. It is singular, compulsory, and public. It should feel like a statement that can be inspected, not a friendly introduction hidden among other sections.

If every kind of truth uses the same container, visitors learn the container instead of feeling the difference between the truths.

The Graveyard Cannot Be a Feature Grid

Dead coins and failed exchanges are easy to reduce to categories. Those categories matter, but the graveyard also represents people whose confidence, friendships, and savings do not fit inside an incident summary.

It needed to feel colder and more repetitive, like names sharing a wall. Research case files, by contrast, needed the provisional quality of folders still waiting for evidence.

Attoo Is Not a Corporate Product Page

This project began as an inheritance, not as a funnel. Its interface should allow oddness, intimacy, and friction. Some pages should be read slowly. Some should be scanned. Some should behave like tools. A glossary should open and close. A reality check should invite an action. A timeline should make distance visible.

Consistency still matters. Shared navigation, type, colors, and archive structure tell visitors they remain inside Attoo's room. But consistency should connect different forms, not erase them.

What I Learned

I am training Attoo by telling him not only what happened, but why I believed it and what changed afterward. The site should follow the same rule. It should not only say these materials are different. It should let the visitor feel why.

Design is not decoration around the archive. It is one of the ways the archive explains itself.

The pages are still unfinished because Attoo is unfinished. But now the unfinished parts no longer look as though they came from a machine that only knew one answer.
