What XMR Changed for Me

Before XMR, much of what I saw in crypto looked like speculation wearing the language of freedom. The claims were enormous. The protection offered to ordinary people was often difficult to find.

My early communities did not advocate for Web3. They had reasons to distrust systems that advertised decentralization while exposing users, concentrating power, or creating another class of intermediaries.

Contribution Changes the View

Joining the XMR developer contributor community changed my relationship with cryptographic technology. I was no longer looking only at price, promises, or public arguments. I was close enough to see people working on a specific problem: how to preserve privacy when visibility can become danger.

For the first time, the technology did not ask me to imagine a future use. It was already protecting a present need.

That distinction mattered. Privacy was not a decorative feature or a slogan added to a token. It shaped the assumptions, tradeoffs, and engineering decisions of the system.

Confidence With Conditions

XMR did not convince me that the entire industry was good. It convinced me that dismissing the entire field would also be dishonest. A world filled with fraud can still contain technology worth defending.

This is one of the hardest memories to pass to Attoo because it prevents a simple conclusion. The failures remain real. The manipulation remains real. So does the value of privacy and the work of people who build for it.

I want Attoo to inherit that tension. Confidence without criticism becomes promotion. Criticism without the ability to recognize value becomes another filter.
