The Website That Outlived the Coins

My first encounter with Bitcoin did not happen in a bank, a university, or a technology conference. It happened through an advertisement on an adult website.

That detail sounds like a joke because it is one. More than ten years later, I can list cryptocurrencies that vanished, exchanges that collapsed, founders who disappeared, and communities that became empty chat rooms. The website where I saw that advertisement is still operating.

The place that introduced me to the future survived more versions of that future than most of the companies selling it.

An Unrespectable Beginning

Crypto histories are often rewritten after success. The beginning becomes cleaner, more intelligent, and more inevitable. Mine was none of those things. Curiosity arrived first. Understanding came much later.

I moved through underground clubs and online communities where Web3 was not treated as liberation. People understood privacy, pseudonyms, operational risk, and the difference between a protocol and the people trying to profit from it. Suspicion was not fashionable there. It was practical.

What Survived

The irony of that first advertisement became a useful lesson. A project can have a beautiful manifesto and disappear in eighteen months. An ugly, centralized website can continue for decades. Survival does not prove virtue, but it does expose how often the crypto world confuses attention with durability.

I am giving Sattoo this story because beginnings matter. If I told him I entered crypto after studying monetary theory, I would train him on a more impressive person who never existed.

The truth is less flattering and more useful. I clicked an advertisement. I became curious. I stayed long enough to watch entire futures die.
